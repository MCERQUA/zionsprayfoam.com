# Blog: Does Spray Foam Insulation Lower Home Value? Separating Fact from Myth

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Meta title:** Does Spray Foam Insulation Lower Home Value?
**Meta description:** Where the "spray foam lowers home value" concern actually comes from, what real appraisers and lenders look for, and how to make sure your insulation is an asset, not a liability.

## Where This Question Comes From

"Does spray foam insulation lower home value?" is a common enough search that it's clearly a real concern for a lot of homeowners considering the upgrade, and the honest answer is: it depends entirely on how it was installed, not on the material itself. Understanding where this concern actually comes from helps separate a real risk from an internet myth.

## The Real Source of the Concern: Poor Installation, Not the Material

Almost every documented case of spray foam causing home value or sale problems traces back to installation issues, not the foam itself. Common culprits include foam installed over an existing moisture problem without addressing the moisture first, roofline (unvented) applications that weren't properly detailed for ventilation and led to trapped moisture, and foam applied by an inexperienced installer that ended up patchy, too thin, or improperly cured. In each case, the foam isn't the problem — poor workmanship is, and poor workmanship in any building material can create the same kind of issue.

## What Appraisers and Inspectors Actually Look For

A home inspector or appraiser evaluating spray foam insulation isn't concerned with the material as a category — they're looking for signs of a problem: visible moisture damage, signs of inadequate ventilation in an unvented roof assembly, or foam that looks obviously poorly applied (gaps, inconsistent thickness, discoloration). Properly installed spray foam that shows none of these signs is generally viewed as a positive, not a red flag, since it signals a genuine energy-efficiency upgrade.

## Why Properly Installed Spray Foam Is Usually a Value-Add

Energy efficiency upgrades are broadly recognized as adding value to a home, and spray foam insulation is one of the more effective upgrades available specifically because it addresses both thermal performance and air sealing at once. Buyers and appraisers increasingly recognize spray foam as a premium insulation choice, not a liability, particularly in a climate like southern Utah's where energy costs are a real, ongoing consideration for both cooling and heating.

## The Roofline/Unvented Attic Nuance

The one area where spray foam has a legitimate, documented history of causing problems is in unvented (conditioned) roof assemblies that weren't designed or installed correctly — if the roof assembly isn't detailed properly for the local climate and building code, moisture can get trapped between roofing materials and the foam, leading to real damage over time. This isn't a reason to avoid roofline spray foam altogether; it's a reason to make sure whoever installs it understands proper unvented assembly design for your specific climate and follows it correctly.

## How to Make Sure Your Installation Is an Asset, Not a Risk

A few things protect you: working with an installer who assesses moisture and ventilation conditions before spraying rather than treating every job identically, making sure roofline or unvented attic applications are properly detailed for your local climate and code, and keeping documentation of what was installed, when, and by whom — which is useful both for warranty purposes and for a future buyer's peace of mind.

## What to Tell a Buyer (or Ask a Seller) About Spray Foam

If you're selling a home with spray foam insulation, being able to say when it was installed, by whom, and that it's shown no signs of moisture or performance issues addresses the concern directly. If you're buying a home with existing spray foam, asking those same questions — and having an inspector specifically look for the warning signs described above — is a reasonable, standard step, not a reason to walk away from an otherwise good property.

## The Bottom Line

Spray foam insulation, installed correctly, is broadly recognized as a value-adding upgrade, not a liability. The "lowers home value" concern is real, but it's a proxy for poor installation, not a property of the material itself — which is exactly why choosing an experienced installer who assesses your specific space before recommending a product matters as much as the material choice itself.
